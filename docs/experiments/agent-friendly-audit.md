## Two architectural shapes

Both shapes use the approved matching operation, keep SQL and eligibility deterministic, preserve the existing evaluator for comparison and use the same evidence, weights and ordering rules. Neither introduces another chat agent or provider framework.

| Shape | Interface and owner | Benefit | Cost and failure mode |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A. One retained ranking record with copied consumer projections | The new matching fit operation owns criterion assessments, contributions and internal fit results. A single ordering function derives the final ordered record and its deciding reasons from fit, eligibility and explicit sort. Cards, experiment output, model explanation and fallback read projections of that same record. | Score, reason and evidence cannot be updated separately inside the owning module. Comparison output and explanation use the same source. | Needs a result/data-shape migration when adopted. Eligibility verdicts are inputs; matching must not interpret renter qualification or import buyer/HTTP types. The experiment must define the final ordering owner explicitly rather than duplicate its comparator later. |
| B. Caller-owned public score records | Matching returns assessments and contributions; buyer and the experiment sort public records and construct their own reasons and packets. | Small initial operation; familiar DTOs; buyer retains all existing orchestration. | Callers learn more policy. Separate comparator/projection code can drift. Exported score and rank fields can disagree with their evidence. A reusable pure finalizer reduces this risk but moves the design toward A. |

Prefer A for the retained record and projections. Keep conversation state and retrieval in buyer. Keep provider adapters unchanged. During the experiment the reusable operation remains in `internal/matching`, as the brief requires; existing `Evaluate` and app callers remain controls. Adoption should replace the application's old preference/ranking policy in one scoped migration, then delete the experiment's duplicate orchestration if it is no longer useful. Do not restore `BuildShortlist` as another parked production operation.

## Compact appendable plan

The architecture audit found three recurring classes with independent historical incidents: model-generated facts exceeding authoritative input, unused alternative application paths, and startup accepting unresolved prerequisites. Six historical behavioral regressions failed before their repairs and passed after repair and on current HEAD. Deleted `search.Repository` and `buyer.BuildShortlist` references now fail compilation. Startup and generic-amenity repairs are present; they do not need another redesign.

The remaining priorities are concrete. Buyer drops criterion assessments and deciding keys before constructing its writer packet, so the writer cannot inspect why a score or a stable tie decided the order. SQL regressions still call an unused `Store.Search` path while the application calls `Candidates`. Align useful retrieval proofs with `Candidates` before deleting that path. Add one retained fit/ranking record and derive experiment, card and explanation projections from it. A disposable prototype shows copied projections preserve the authority while public caller-owned score records can diverge from their evidence.

Execute the matching comparison after approval of its brief, with frozen assessments and same-machine measurements. The SQL verification migration is separately approved broader cleanup and does not block that standalone experiment. App integration follows a separate adoption decision and removes superseded policy paths. Actual model prose, disposable-database behavior and live inference performance remain unverified. This audit does not authorize production refactoring or publication. After plan approval, choose the adapted local stack or full autopilot playbook before execution.
